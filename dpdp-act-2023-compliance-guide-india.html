1. Breaking News: MeitY Launches Consent Manager Portal (November 2026)

On November 13, 2026, marking exactly twelve months since the formal notification of the DPDP Rules, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) Secretary, Shri S. Krishnan, officially launched the online registration portal for Consent Managers at dpbi.gov.in.

This landmark milestone operationalizes Phase 2 of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act implementation roadmap. Consent Managers represent a revolutionary institutional layer in India's digital economy: specialized interoperable entities that allow Indian citizens (Data Principals) to grant, review, manage, and revoke their digital consent across multiple companies through a unified, transparent dashboard.

Under the newly operational framework, entities incorporated in India with a minimum net worth of ₹2 crore, robust technical infrastructure, and zero conflict of interest can register with the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI). For enterprises, software developers, and business owners in Maharashtra, this announcement signals that the grace period is rapidly closing, and the final May 2027 enforcement deadline for full compliance is fast approaching.

2. Core Architecture of the DPDP Act: Roles & Legal Responsibilities

To achieve genuine compliance rather than superficial paperwork, Indian business owners must understand the statutory entities defined under the DPDP Act:

  • Data Principal: The individual to whom the personal data relates (your website visitor, customer, employee, or app user). If the individual is a child (under 18) or a person with disability, their lawful guardian is the Data Principal.
  • Data Fiduciary: Any person, company, or enterprise that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. If your company operates an e-commerce store, CRM, mobile app, or billing system in Pune, your business is a Data Fiduciary.
  • Data Processor: Any third-party entity that processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary (e.g., cloud hosting providers, SMS gateways, payment aggregators).
  • Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF): High-volume or sensitive entities designated by the Central Government based on data volume, risk to electoral democracy, or state security. SDFs must appoint a resident Data Protection Officer (DPO), conduct periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs), and undergo independent compliance audits.

4. Penalties Up to ₹250 Crore: Understanding Legal Liabilities

Unlike previous IT regulations that lacked teeth, the DPDP Act establishes the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) as a digital-by-default adjudicatory body empowered to impose unprecedented financial penalties under Schedule to Section 33:

Up to ₹250 Crore Fine

For failure by a Data Fiduciary to implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach under Section 8(5).

Up to ₹200 Crore Fine

For failure to notify the Board and affected Data Principals of a personal data breach under Section 8(6), or breach of obligations regarding children's data under Section 9.

The Act specifically notes that penalties are calculated based on the nature, gravity, and duration of the violation, the repetitive nature of defaults, and the financial turnover of the enterprise. For SMEs, even a fraction of these penalties would prove catastrophic.
